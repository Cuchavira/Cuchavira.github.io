# Touch-to-Meaning Research Focus, Concept Diagram, and Revised RQs

Date: 2026-06-15

Purpose: reframe the current research focus, organize the key entities and relationships, and rewrite the research questions in an HCI-compatible form.

## 1. Updated Research Focus

Touch-to-Meaning is a qualitative HCI and learning-sciences study of how tactile-material episodes make neurodivergent learners' participation, expression, and clock-time sense-making observable during a structured handcraft activity.

The study does not primarily test whether students learned clock concepts better. Instead, it asks how material actions, multimodal expressions, artifact traces, and educator interpretation can be linked into traceable descriptions of participation and meaning-making.

## 2. Terminology Decisions

| Original wording | Recommended term | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learning content: time and clock | Clock-time learning context | Keeps the activity grounded in time/clock concepts without claiming a full learning-outcome study. |
| Recognize clock | Clock recognition | Standard, concise learning-content term. |
| Telling time on the hour | Telling time to the hour | More natural phrasing for the target concept. |
| One-day time plan | Daily time planning | Broader and more natural than one-day time plan. |
| Structured learning activity | Structured clock-time handcraft activity | Names both the learning content and the making format. |
| Handicraft making | Handcraft making | More natural in HCI/design writing. |
| Physical material library | Tactile-material library | Connects materials directly to the project's tactile focus. |
| Tactile preference/interaction | Tactile-material engagement patterns | Avoids overclaiming stable preference from short observations. |
| Expression, observed behaviors | Multimodal expression and observable action | Standard HCI/video-analysis framing. |
| Skill of operation/action | Fine-motor and tool-use actions | Captures tool use, manipulation, and action skill without sounding clinical. |
| Sensory profile/regulation | Sensory profile and regulation needs | Keeps sensory differences situated and support-oriented. |
| Engagement/attention | Engagement and participation trajectory | More process-oriented than attention alone. |
| Completion of artefacts | Artifact completion and process traces | Includes both final product and making process. |
| Understanding of concepts | Clock-time sense-making evidence | Avoids claiming formal concept mastery without assessment data. |
| Interpretation/observation | Educator observation and interpretation | Clarifies that teacher perspective is an interpretive layer. |
| Assistance/teaching | Scaffolding and pedagogical response | Standard learning-sciences and HCI classroom terminology. |
| Adjust/design curriculum | Curriculum and material adaptation | Links teacher action to future design implications. |

## 5. How the Diagram Reframes the Research Problem

The diagram now works as a four-module map rather than a dense interaction network. Each module names one part of the research structure, and the RQ layer below uses trace chips, such as `A4` or `D2`, to show which exact sub-items each question draws on.

1. Module A defines the structured clock-time activity context.
2. Module B defines learner sensory-motor and interaction profiles.
3. Module C defines observable participation and task-linked learning evidence.
4. Module D defines educator interpretation and pedagogical response.

This framing supports a qualitative framework contribution: a way to describe and interpret tactile-material episodes in neurodivergent learning contexts without overclaiming formal learning outcomes.

## 6. Revised Research Questions

| RQ | Revised question | Primary evidence | Claim boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RQ1 | How do neurodivergent learners engage with tactile materials during structured learning activities? | Video episodes, field notes, material actions, artifact process traces | Can describe situated engagement patterns, but not stable tactile preferences from single episodes. |
| RQ2 | How can observable actions, expressions, and artifact traces be described as evidence of participation and meaning-making? | Low-inference observation, timestamps, photos, field notes, work IDs, uncertainty notes | Descriptions must separate observation from interpretation and include evidence links. |
| RQ3 | How do educators interpret and respond to tactile-material episodes in ways that support learner participation? | Teacher labels, teacher interview, support actions, selected clip review, before/after episode context | Teacher interpretation is contextual evidence, not a substitute for learner evidence. |
| Follow-up validation question | To what extent do candidate tactile-material descriptions recur or change across sessions, evidence sources, and educator reflections? | Cross-session recurrence, cross-source links, teacher confirmation or disagreement | This should remain follow-up validation unless repeated-session evidence becomes strong enough for a full RQ. |

## 7. RQ Logic

The three main RQs move from observation to interpretation to educational response:

1. RQ1 asks how tactile-material engagement appears during structured learning activities.
2. RQ2 asks how observable episodes can become evidence-linked descriptions of participation and meaning-making.
3. RQ3 asks how teachers interpret and respond to those episodes in support of learner participation.

The follow-up validation question protects the study from overclaiming salience or stability before recurrence and change across sessions are verified.

## 8. Short Version for Proposal or Supervisor Discussion

This project studies how neurodivergent learners' tactile-material actions during a clock-making activity can be observed, described, and interpreted as evidence of participation and clock-time sense-making. The contribution is an HCI framework for tracing tactile-material episodes from visible action and artifact traces to educator interpretation and future pedagogical design, while keeping learning-outcome claims bounded by the available evidence.

## 9. Acceptance Checks

- Each RQ maps to available evidence from video, field notes, artifacts, and teacher interpretation.
- The wording foregrounds interaction, traceability, interpretation, and scaffolding, which fits HCI qualitative research.
- The framing avoids deficit-only language and avoids treating sensory differences as problems to correct.
- The follow-up validation question keeps salience/stability separate from the three core RQs until repeated-session evidence is stronger.
