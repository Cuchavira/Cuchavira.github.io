2. Project Overview

2.1 One-paragraph Summary

This protocol template describes an observation-based classroom workshop for Touch-to-Meaning. Eight students work in four pairs on a traditional handcraft activity about understanding clocks, numbers, and time. One main teacher, three assistant teachers, and three on-site researchers support and observe the workshop. Data sources include field notes, teacher interview, group 360 video, photos, audio, and student work. The protocol’s paper-methods purpose is to verify whether these data sources and the resulting analysis outputs can be mapped coherently to the study RQs.

4. Research Questions

4.1 RQ Design Logic

These research questions move from observing tactile response patterns, to translating multimodal expression, to tracing educator interpretation, and then building a framework.

For this protocol version, use four research questions. RQ4 is a multi-session salience/stability question: the June 2 workshop can identify candidate semantic descriptions and test the evidence structure, but it cannot by itself prove salience across sessions.

4.2 Working Primary RQs

RQ ID Working wording Primary construct Status
RQ1: Tactile response patterns What tactile response patterns emerge when neurodivergent learners engage with tactile materials in a structured learning activity? Observable material choice, touch/manipulation, task execution, and pair/individual variation. Working
RQ2: Traceable semantic descriptions How can learners’ multimodal expressions around tactile episodes be translated into traceable semantic descriptions of tactile experience? Translation from multimodal expression to evidence-linked semantic description, with uncertainty preserved. Working
RQ3: Educator interpretation What educator knowledge is needed to interpret and respond to learners’ tactile expressions? Teacher labels, classroom routines, support decisions, and misread-risk boundaries. Working
RQ4: Salience of semantic descriptions How salient and stable are the semantic descriptions of tactile experience across multiple sessions? Recurrence, cross-source support, cross-session stability, and framework-building value. Provisional / requires multiple sessions

4.3 Domain-Specific Subquestion About Clock/Time Learning

Do not make a broad main RQ such as “What is the relationship between tactile experience and abstract number sense/time perception?” unless the study has enough sessions and learning assessment evidence to support that claim.

For this workshop, use a narrower task-context subquestion:

Subquestion Placement Evidence needed
How do tactile-material handcraft actions make students’ engagement with clock numbers and time representation observable during the activity? Analytic subquestion under RQ1, RQ2, and RQ3; contextual evidence for RQ4 if descriptions recur across sessions. Group 360 video, field notes, student work, teacher behavior labels, teacher interview, and clock/time task codes.

4.4 RQ4 Salience Boundary

RQ4 asks how salient and stable semantic descriptions of tactile experience are across multiple sessions. A single 30-minute workshop can only generate candidate descriptions and test whether the evidence structure can support later salience claims.

  • A semantic description is more salient if it appears across multiple evidence sources, such as observed action, student expression, teacher labels, teacher interpretation, and student work context.
  • A semantic description is more stable if it recurs across multiple sessions without depending on one ambiguous adult inference.
  • A semantic description is less stable if it appears only once, only through adult inference, or only through ambiguous video evidence.
  • Testing salience and stability requires the later June sessions and should be written as a provisional finding until repeated-session evidence is coded.

7. Materials, Prototype, and Activity

7.1 Learning Activity Anchor

Understanding clocks, numbers, and time through a traditional handcraft workshop.

Workshop task: students make a “beautiful clock” through a traditional handcraft activity. Students are divided into three task levels based on ability:

Level Difficulty Clock-making task focus Support assumption
A Highest Student completes the most open-ended version: selects materials, composes the clock face, places or marks number points, and creates/positions the clock hands with minimal prompting. Teacher support mainly for instruction clarification, safety, and time management.
B Medium Student completes a partially scaffolded version: chooses among prepared options, places key numbers or number points, decorates the clock face, and positions hands with some guidance. Teacher support may include modeling, step reminders, and material assistance.
C Highest support Student completes a simplified version: chooses or handles materials, decorates or attaches larger prepared components, and participates in number/time representation through supported action. Teacher support may include hand-over-hand only if normally used and ethically appropriate, plus simplified choices and repeated prompts.

Expected output: each student or pair produces a handcraft clock artifact. [TO CONFIRM: individual artifact or pair artifact]

10. Teacher Interview and Contextual Interpretation

10.1 Interpretation Principle

This workshop is observation based. Do not require learner interviews or post-hoc explanations unless they naturally occur within normal classroom interaction and are approved by the protocol. Teacher interview data should be used to contextualize observed participation, support strategies, classroom routines, and possible meanings of student actions. Teacher interpretation must remain analytically separate from directly observed student action and student-produced work.

10.4 Prompts to Avoid

Avoid prompts that ask teachers to diagnose students, rank students, infer internal states with certainty, or describe behavior as failure/noncompliance without context. Also avoid asking students direct “why” questions during the workshop unless the teacher considers the question normal, accessible, and non-pressuring in that classroom.
