Step 1. RQ Unpacking Before Data Mapping

Research questions:

These research questions move from observing tactile response patterns, to translating multimodal expression, to tracing educator interpretation, and then building a framework.

RQ Short Name Question
RQ1 Tactile response patterns What tactile response patterns emerge when neurodivergent learners engage with tactile materials in a structured learning activity?
RQ2 Traceable semantic descriptions How can learners’ multimodal expressions around tactile episodes be translated into traceable semantic descriptions of tactile experience?
RQ3 Educator interpretation What educator knowledge is needed to interpret and respond to learners’ tactile expressions?
RQ4 Salience of semantic descriptions How salient and stable are the semantic descriptions of tactile experience across multiple sessions?

Use the table below as the evidence contract before splitting the interview into nodes. “Prove” means “support with traceable evidence,” not causal proof. RQ4 requires repeated-session evidence; the June 2 workshop can only identify candidate descriptions and test the mapping structure.

RQ Elements that need evidence Direct evidence to collect Field notes focus Teacher/context evidence needed
RQ1: Tactile response patterns Material choice, touch/manipulation type, task execution pattern, task-stage context, pair or individual variation. Front/tabletop/top-down views from group 360 video, photos, and student work process traces. Record time range, task segment, material involved, visible hand/material action, repetition or avoidance, group/student code, and evidence source. Teacher labels for whether an action is typical, unusual, preference-like, avoidance-like, help-seeking, or task-driven.
RQ2: Traceable semantic descriptions Observable multimodal expression, candidate semantic description, source link, uncertainty, possible alternative meanings. Video/audio segment, photo, student work, or field-note episode tied to the observed expression. Separate observed action/expression, possible semantic description, evidence link, confidence level, and teacher follow-up question. Teacher explanation only when it helps interpret meaning; teacher interpretation must remain separate from direct observation.
RQ3: Educator interpretation Teacher label, classroom routine, learner history known to the teacher, support response, misread risk. Teacher feedback/interview, selected video clips, behavior labels, and field-note follow-up questions. Note what requires educator interpretation and what support/response happened. Teacher explanation of classroom meaning, support needs, behavior label, or why a researcher might misread the moment.
RQ4: Salience of semantic descriptions Recurrence across sources, recurrence across sessions, consistency or contradiction, framework relevance. Repeated links across field notes, video, work/photos, and teacher interpretation over multiple sessions. Mark whether a description appears once, across sources, or across sessions. Teacher confirmation, disagreement, or refinement across sessions; mark when evidence is too thin for salience claims.

Step 6. Analysis Pipeline

  1. Organize data by session, student, pair/group, and 360 camera file.
  2. Create NVivo cases for students, pairs, teachers, and sessions.
  3. Add persona fields as student case attributes.
  4. Segment videos into meaningful tactile/material episodes.
  5. Code group 360 video for hand-material actions, artifact-making, orientation when visible, peer interaction, and teacher support.
  6. Use field notes, photos, audio, and student work to supplement video evidence.
  7. Ask all teachers to label selected clips.
  8. Link each interpretation to timestamped evidence.
  9. Add teacher-rated knowledge scores after scale is confirmed.
  10. Build the final RQ-data-results matrix.
  11. Remove any claim that has no direct evidence link.
