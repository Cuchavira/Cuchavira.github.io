TicTac: Mapping Neurodivergent Learners’ Tactile Experiences into Semantic Vocabulary for Future Adaptive Tangible AI

Current status

Overall for project

This project investigates how neurodivergent learners express tactile/haptic experience during hands-on learning, especially when visible actions cannot be read as direct evidence of preference or comfort. The project aims to build traceable ways to connect tactile episodes, learner expression, educator interpretation, and future adaptive tangible design.

Core question for the project

Research goals

Research goal: develop a grounded account of how tactile experience becomes meaningful in classroom interaction, then use that account to inform educator interpretation and future adaptive tangible design.

Research questions

These research questions move from observing tactile response patterns, to translating multimodal expression, to tracing educator interpretation, and then building a framework.

RQ1: What tactile response patterns emerge when neurodivergent learners engage with tactile materials in a structured learning activity?

RQ2: How can learners' multimodal expressions around tactile episodes be translated into traceable semantic descriptions of tactile experience?

RQ3: What educator knowledge is needed to interpret and respond to learners' tactile expressions?

RQ4: How salient and stable are the semantic descriptions of tactile experience across multiple sessions?

Overall method

The current method is an observation-based classroom workshop and follow-up data-organization workflow. The June 2 tactile clock-making activity will generate traceable classroom episodes.

June 2 design probe workshop

The June 2 design probe workshop tests the RQ-data protocol through a 30-minute tactile clock-making activity. The goal is not to prove learning impact or salience, but to collect traceable evidence about tactile/material actions, student expression, teacher interpretation, and candidate semantic descriptions.

The workshop preparation package now contains four priority components:

a 30-minute tactile clock-making task flow;

researcher role instructions for Hongni, Qiyuan, and Xinyu;

field-note, teacher-labeling, and interview-anchor structures;

a data-source plan linking recorded videos, photos/student work, field notes, and teacher context evidence.

Example: the learner-facing path is to explore materials, make or complete a tactile clock face, place/check clock numbers, adjust the clock hands, decorate or repair the work, and express stop, pause, repeat, change, help, or finish through ordinary classroom interaction where available.
