Project: Touch-to-Meaning Version: Researcher version Activity: One-Day Timeline: Day/Night and Morning/Noon/Afternoon Time: June 16, 2026 Participants: lead teacher, available assistant teachers, 4 observers, 7 participating students Observer team: Qiyuan, Xingyu, Mengxu, Hongni; Duration: About 30 minutes

1. Goals

This workshop extends the previous time-themed classroom sessions from clocks and watch/alarm making to the structure of a day. Students are introduced to a one-day timeline, day/night, and morning/noon/afternoon, then make sun or moon artifacts.

  • Observe how students respond to the one-day timeline and time-period concepts.
  • Observe how students use tactile and craft materials while making sun/moon artifacts.
  • Record student expression, teacher support.
  • Keep artifacts, field notes, camera records, group roles, and consent boundaries linkable after class.

4. Flexible Activity Flow

The main flow is to introduce a one-day timeline and then make a sun or moon artifact. Stages 0-2 now form one concept-introduction stage. Stage 3 is unpacked into two making options: sun or moon. The stages are classroom orientation labels and field-note anchors, not strict time blocks.

Stage Label Student-facing action Observation focus
0-2 Concept Introduction 0 view the one-day timeline; 1 distinguish day/night; 2 place morning/noon/afternoon on the one-day timeline. Starting state, attention to model, concept response, matching, life-context references, teacher support, interpretation boundary.
3A Sun-Making Option Paste cardstock, draw, and roll cardstock strips. Material choice, making strategy, persistence, repair, substitution, peer or teacher influence.
3B Moon-Making Option Paste pieces and attach round pieces onto the moon shape. Material choice, fine-motor access, placement strategy, support needed, substitution, peer or teacher influence.
4 Show and Express Show the work, indicate done/continue/help/change/pause/stop, or respond to teacher questions. Multimodal expression, teacher interpretation, artifact-state evidence.

7. Post-Class Organization

  • Confirm every observation table has observer notes, even if notes are short.
  • Create the group-level camera-file mapping table before detailed video review.
  • O2 photographs each final work and links it to work ID, group, student code, field-note references, and camera time range where possible.
  • O3 completes the equipment return checklist before leaving the room.
  • O4 transfers every SD card to both hard drives and verifies file count and total size for each card.
  • Keep isolated concept responses as candidates until teacher interpretation, video evidence, or cross-source evidence supports them.
  • Summarize 2-3 key moments per group, especially moments where time concept, material choice, support, or expression needs teacher interpretation.

8. Minimum Success Criteria

  • Each student completes, nearly completes, or meaningfully participates in making a sun/moon artifact.
  • Each group has a traceable observer note, camera-file mapping, and work/photo link.
  • O2/O3/O4 complete artifact photos, equipment return, and two-drive data transfer before the team leaves or before any SD card is cleared.
  • The team can tell which stage label a field-note episode belongs to without forcing a rigid stage interpretation.
  • The next workshop adjustment can be stated clearly in materials, classroom setup, support method, or observation focus.
