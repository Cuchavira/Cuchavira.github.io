Fitzgerald, Hackling, & Dawson (2013)

Core research focus: How video can be collected and analyzed in classroom research, especially when studying teaching and learning practices over time.

Notes-taken: - Video ethnography: - To develop a more holistic understanding, there needs to be conversation and negotiation between participant and researcher. - Ethics to maintian: - copyright issues and human participant ethics considerations, such as access to schools, - the identification of students, the use of video footage outside of the classroom, - the short-term and long-term implications of this footage on the participants, - the development of understandings around who decides who views the video footage, - the existence of a permanent record of teacher and student actions. - Focal Study - two primary school teachers - weekly observations (10 weeks) - Techniques - FM microphnes - one with a wide-angle lens - two with regular lenses mounted on tripods - Data Collecting - Sampling - simplicity is the key - Authenticity - acting for the camera (teachers and students) - Ethics - develop protocols for preventing harm, particularly in the form of embarrassing events or actions captured - Analyzing and Interpreting Video Data - analyzed data source - video footage, - transcripts of the interviews with the teachers, interviews with the focus group students, - artefacts collected from the classroom, - journal entries relating to the research process. - triangulation - ensure the credibility, transferability, dependability, confirmability, and authenticity of the data - Analytic approach - Microanalysis: - an event or behaviour can be described, measured, or tracked in detail through repeated examination of video sequences. - 5-steps: 1. watching a video sequence in its entirety, 2. identifying major events within the sequence, 3. looking at the links between event segments, 4. transcribing the interactions,
5. comparing segments across the video data set - Interpretation - positioning, neutrality, and objectivity of the research process

Core finding: Video can provide a permanent, revisitable, multi-perspective record of classroom practice, but its usefulness depends on careful sampling, technical planning, authenticity management, ethical protocols, and triangulation.

Methodological contribution: This paper contributes the strongest practical guidance for recording design: camera placement, wide versus close framing, audio setup, no-go zones, participant reactivity, consent, confidentiality, and member checking.

McNaughton (2009)

Core research focus: How video recordings of classroom drama lessons can be processed and analyzed to understand interaction, discourse, and learning in education for sustainable development (ESD drame, role-play).

Methodological contribution: This paper is the procedural backbone. It provides a five-stage workflow, a transcription key, verbal/nonverbal discourse categories, and a transcription-analysis matrix.

Notes: - Project context & class setting - 5 sub cases (3 classes, 2 episodes (lessons sets)) - lessons (two sets, about drama) - Research questiosn - which specific ESD learning outcomes,
- (concepts, skills, values, positive attitudes, and sympathy/empathy with others), might be addressed through the medium of drama? - which drama techniques and conventions - could be employed to help children to explore issues and to extend their learning in ESD. - what the actions and interactions (both pupils and teacher) indicated about how and why drama can be a useful tool in teaching and learning in ESD? - 5 stages - Stage 1: Creating logs of the video data - the number of participants, the type, teacher involvement, the nature of the interactions (planning, negotiating, instructing, or information giving), and the subject matter, - cross-referenced : interview + field notes - Stage 2: Initial identification of significant episodes - editing tools: timing, episodes, individual frames - criteria of final selection based on field notes. - - Stage 3: Selection and transcription of episodes for close analysis - contextualization cues (feature of language that contributes to people’s interaction with each other in ways that can be understood.) - - Stage 4: Description and interpretation of the transcribed episodes - 9 cats of verbal disclosure - 6 categories of non-verbal disclosure : - facial expression, gaze, gesture, posture, touch, and spatial behaviors/proxemics - Stage 5: Seeking themes and making links to learning in ESD - coded themes - Organization/ summary analysis of thematic codes

Transcriptions and interpretative comments matrix: the analysis instrumentation > there is no single correct interpretation of events. The analyst’s purpose and perspectives will have a bearing on what is deemed to be relevant or important.

  • Spiers’s (2004) structure, followed by a reflective commentary
  • Standard Jeffersonian Transcription Notation: https://universitytranscriptions.co.uk/jefferson-transcription-system-a-guide-to-the-symbols/
