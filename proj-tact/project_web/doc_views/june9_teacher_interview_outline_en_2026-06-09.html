June 9 Brief Post-Class Teacher Interview Outline

Project: Touch-to-Meaning

Activity: Everyday watch / alarm-clock making activity

Date: June 9, 2026

Format: Brief semi-structured post-class interview

Suggested length: 10-15 minutes

Participants: Lead teacher and assistant teachers who participated in this classroom activity

Interview lead: O1

Interview Purpose

This interview does not repeat the full 30-minute teacher interview. Instead, it is a quick confirmation focused on the new June 9 activity design. The focus is to ask teachers to help the research team understand specific classroom moments and judge whether the more flexible “everyday watch / alarm-clock” making format improved task entry, material handling, teacher support, and student expression.

Teacher explanations will be used as classroom-context evidence to help the research team interpret video, field notes, and student work. Teacher explanations do not replace evidence from students’ own actions, expressions, and artifacts.

Opening Script

  • Thank the teachers for participating in and supporting today’s classroom activity.
  • Explain that this is not a class evaluation and not an assessment of student learning outcomes.
  • Explain that the main goal is to ask teachers to help interpret several classroom moments and confirm how the next activity should be adjusted.
  • If possible, ask teachers to answer with reference to specific students, groups, materials, actions, or classroom episodes.

Interview Questions

1. Activity Format

Today the activity changed from a fixed “make a clock” task to a more flexible “make an everyday watch / alarm clock” task. Was it easier for students to enter the task? Please give one specific student or group example.

2. Key Episode

Which episode best showed a student’s material preference, participation state, or support need? What did the student do at that moment? How did the teacher judge it?

3. Task Difficulty and Differentiation

Last time, teachers mentioned that the task difficulty needed to be lowered for some students. Were today’s materials and task differentiation more appropriate? Which parts were still too difficult?

4. Material Adjustment

Which materials from today should be kept, reduced, or replaced? Please explain from three angles: safety, sensory comfort, and operation difficulty.

5. Time-Setting Segment

During the “set the time” segment, did students show engagement with the time concept? If not, was the main difficulty conceptual understanding, physical operation, or attention / emotional state?

6. Student Expression

Which students expressed “continue,” “do not want,” “need help,” “finished,” or “want to change materials” today? Were these expressions verbal, gestural, facial, or body-state based?

7. Behaviors Researchers Might Misread

Was there any behavior that researchers would easily misunderstand if they only watched the video? How would teachers explain that behavior?

8. Priority for the Next Adjustment

If a similar activity continues next time, what should be adjusted first: materials, task steps, teacher support, or observation focus?

Closing Notes

  • Ask each teacher to add one most important observation or suggestion.
  • O1 records episodes that need later checking against video or field notes.
  • If specific video episodes need confirmation later, arrange a short clip-review session.

O1 Recording Reminders

During the interview, record the following information where possible:

  • Specific student or group code.
  • Corresponding activity stage: material exploration, making and decoration, time setting, showing and expression.
  • Specific material, action, and teacher support strategy.
  • The teacher’s basis for interpretation.
  • Places where researchers might misread the episode or where evidence is insufficient.
  • Specific suggestions for modifying the next activity.
