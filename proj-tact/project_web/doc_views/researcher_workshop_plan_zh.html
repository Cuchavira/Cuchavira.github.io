Touch-to-Meaning Tactile Classroom Workshop Plan (Researcher Version)

Project: Touch-to-Meaning Version: Researcher version Activity: Learning Everyday Clocks and Making Tactile Clocks Time: June 2, 2026, 14:15 Participants: 3 teachers, 7 students, 3 researchers Duration: About 30 minutes Format: Classroom activity with teacher support and researcher observation

1. Goals

This activity combines everyday clock learning with tactile making. Students create a tactile clock by choosing, touching, placing, moving, and decorating materials. The research team records participation, material use, expression, and responses to support.

  • Observe how students understand the clock face, number placement, hand direction, and whole-hour time.
  • Observe how students choose materials, manipulate them, ask for help, and express completion or continuation.
  • Link field notes, camera records, student work photos, and teacher feedback without expanding beyond the confirmed consent boundary.

7. Post-Class Organization

  • Hongni checks consent boundaries, records teacher feedback, marks teacher-confirmed interpretations, and confirms Group 2/DV1 file mapping.
  • Qiyuan stops recording, powers off, removes, and packs the Group 3 360 camera, DV2, and related equipment.
  • Xinyu collects and photographs works, checks work IDs, organizes tabletop materials, and confirms Group 4 camera-file mapping.
  • The research team creates the camera-file mapping table for all group-level 360 camera files and DV files.
  • The research team summarizes 2-3 key material-interaction or support events from each group.

8. Minimum Success Criteria

  • Each student completes or nearly completes a displayable tactile clock.
  • Each group has at least one traceable material-interaction record.
  • The team can link works, field notes, teacher interpretations, recording files, and consent boundaries.
  • The next activity can be adjusted clearly in materials, task steps, support methods, and spatial setup.
