Semi-structured Interview Outline

English Version

Format: a 30-minute semi-structured group interview with the three teachers who attended and supported the hands-on workshop. The interview focuses on classroom moments, student actions, materials, time concepts, teacher support, and next-step suggestions.

Goals of this interview

  • To further understand teachers’ overall experience of the hands-on workshop.

  • To understand and interpret neurodivergent learners’ observed behaviors, material preferences, engagement, and support needs from teachers’ professional classroom perspective.

  • To collect teacher interpretation as observation-based contextual evidence.

What this interview should achieve (Mainly answer RQ2)

  • Identify key classroom moments that teachers consider meaningful for understanding student participation.

  • Clarify how teachers interpreted students’ behaviors, preferences, engagement cues, and needs during the learning session in general. (Q1-2)

  • Connect teacher interpretations to concrete evidence: learning task (how they engage with clock making stages), student action (exploration & manipulation), material (exact students’ preference and work). (Q2-4)

  • Generate practical suggestions for adjusting materials, task difficulty, support strategies, and observation focus in the next activity. (Q6-7)

Participants

  • This interview is for the teachers who attended and supported the hands-on workshop.

0. Opening and Positioning (2-3 min)

  • Thank you for participating.

  • This interview is not an evaluation of the class or teachers. We want to understand the workshop through your professional classroom perspective.

  • How do you feel after the class? Please each describe it in one sentence.

1. Overall Review (4 min)

  • During the craft task, how was the overall completion? Were you satisfied with it?

  • Which students worked most smoothly, and in which stage? Which students faced the most challenge, and in which stage?

2. Concrete Moment (4 min)

  • Please describe one student and one task moment that left the strongest impression on you.

  • You may discuss the student’s exploration and control of materials, material interests and preferences, hands-on ability, and how time concepts were integrated into the making process.

3. Moment-by-Moment Recall (8 min)

  • For this student’s action sequence, what was the first thing you noticed or heard in class?

  • Where did you place your attention: the student’s actions, facial expression, sounds, ways of handling materials, or the position of the work?

  • How did the student touch and manipulate the materials? Which actions did you see most often: rolling, pinching, pressing, repeating, or staying still?

  • How did you respond at that moment? For example: verbal prompt, physical assistance, moving closer, waiting, demonstrating, or replacing a material.

  • How did you judge whether the student liked it, resisted it, needed help, or was thinking? What observable signs led you to that judgment?

  • Which actions do you think are easy to overlook or misunderstand?

4. Learning Points (3 min)

  • How do you think the hands-on clock-making task helped students understand clock-related knowledge?

  • Which students do you think showed stronger understanding? At what level? Which students needed more support?

5. Materials and Learning (3 min)

  • Which materials did you find most popular and most able to stimulate exploration? Which materials might cause distraction?

  • If we continue this activity next time, which materials would you keep, add, or remove from the perspectives of teaching, manipulation, sensory experience, and safety?

6. Expression and Participation (3 min)

  • What student expressions would you understand as “I like it,” “I don’t want it,” “continue,” “I need help,” or “I’m done”?

  • Are there any nonverbal expressions you would pay attention to in class?

7. Suggestions and Closing (3 min)

  • What do you think should be adjusted most in the next activity?

  • Thank you.

中文版

希望通过访谈获得

  • 找出老师认为最能说明学生参与状态的关键课堂片段。

  • 明确老师如何解释学生在学习活动中的行为、偏好、参与线索和需求。

  • 将老师的解释连接到具体证据：任务阶段、学生动作、材料或作品、教师回应和课堂情境。

  • 发现研究者在没有教师专业背景知识时容易忽略或误解的片段。

  • 形成下一次活动在材料、任务难度、支持方式和观察重点上的具体调整建议。

0. 开场与定位（2-3 分钟）

  • 感谢老师参与。

  • 几位老师上完课的感受如何？请用一句话描述一下。

1. 整体回顾（4 分钟）

  • 手工任务过程中，整体完成情况如何？老师满意吗？

  • 哪些学生在哪个环节操作最顺利？哪些学生在哪个环节最有挑战？

2. 具体片段（4 分钟）

  • 请描述一个印象最深刻的学生和任务片段。

  • 可以从材料摸索与掌握、材料兴趣偏好、动手能力、时间概念融入等角度说明。

3. 时间顺序瞬间回顾（8 分钟）

  • 针对这个学生的操作片段，你在当时课堂上最先注意到或听到的内容是什么？

  • 你的注意力主要放在哪里：学生动作、表情、声音、材料操作方式，还是作品位置？

  • 学生是如何接触和操作材料的？哪些动作最频繁：揉、捏、按、重复，还是停住不动？

  • 你当时有哪些回应？例如口头提示、身体协助、靠近、等待、示范，或帮助替换材料。

  • 你当时如何判断学生喜欢、抗拒、需要帮助，通过什么现象推断？（有哪些操作行为容易被忽略或误解？）

4. 知识点相关（3 分钟）

  • 动手制作时钟的任务，可以如何帮助学生理解时钟相关知识？

  • 哪些学生掌握更好？体现在哪个层面？哪些学生需要更多支持？

5. 材料与知识（3 分钟）

  • 哪些材料最受学生欢迎（使用最多），也最能激发探索？哪些材料可能造成分心？

  • 如果下次继续制作，从教学、操作、感统和安全角度看，应保留、增加、删减哪些材料？

6. 表达和参与（3 分钟）

  • 学生哪些表达可以理解为“喜欢、不要、继续、需要帮助、完成了”？

  • 有没有不是语言表达，但也需要在课堂上关注的表现？

7. 建议与结束（3 分钟）

  • 下次活动最需要调整什么？

  • 感谢老师。
